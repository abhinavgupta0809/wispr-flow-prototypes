Two problems from Flow's reviews, one prototype each

People who love Flow's dictation describe two moments where it lets them down: fixing one wrong word on a phone, and trusting that a cleaned-up draft still says everything they said. One is ready to try. The other starts with an experiment.

Prototype 1

Mobile Fix Strip

Problem
Mobile users can enjoy Flow's dictation quality but lose the time-saving benefit when correcting a phrase requires awkward selection, typing, or switching keyboards.
Customer evidence
Proposed
Tap the wrong word in a strip showing your last dictation, say or type the fix, preview it, apply. Every other character stays as it was.
Status
Interactive demo ready. The comparison with Flow's current iOS keyboard waits until it's observed on a phone.

Prototype 2

Thought Check

Problem hypothesis
When spoken thoughts become a cleaned-up draft, users may struggle to notice whether an important detail, condition, or instruction disappeared or changed.
Customer evidence
Method
Ten scripted passages read into the real Flow app, each checked unit by unit for what survived. Then repeats, then two unscripted brain dumps.

Where the evidence comes from

The Wispr Flow app reviews dashboard: 7,007 public Google Play and App Store reviews of the mobile apps, May 2025 to Oct 2026, each labeled by an LLM and checked against 120 hand-labeled reviews.

Quotes on these pages are copied verbatim from its export and identified by review ID. Store reviews are self-selected, so they show that a problem exists for someone. They don't show how common it is.

What's simulated

  • Fix Strip: the dictated sentence and the spoken "Thursday" are prepared text. Nothing is recorded or recognized.
  • Fix Strip: the composer is a mock-up inside this page, not a keyboard that works in other apps.
  • Thought Check's test workspace: nothing is simulated. Every output in it is pasted from the real Flow app.
  • Thought Check's review interaction, once built, will use hand-prepared suggestions, labeled as such.
