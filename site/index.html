More control over how Flow turns speech into writing

Give people more control over how Flow turns speech into writing: quick corrections on mobile, faithful capture for personal reflection, and reviewable choices after complex revisions.

Where Flow is today

Wispr Flow already offers desktop tools for transforming text. This proposal explores how editing can work on mobile and how users could control cleanup and revisit decisions made while speaking.

    Proposed extensions

    Connected ideas, one shared repair panel

    Each is a proposed extension of what Flow ships, not a replacement. They share one Phrase Repair panel: after a mobile dictation, a journal capture, a revision review or a spoken skill command, a wrong word is one tap away.

    They have different amounts of evidence behind them, and each page says how much.

    Phrase Repair

    Grown from Mobile Fix Strip

    Problem
    Mobile users can enjoy Flow's dictation quality but lose the time-saving benefit when correcting a phrase requires awkward selection, typing, or switching keyboards.
    Customer evidence
    My test
    Flow heard “Mobile Fix Strip” as “mobile fix trip.” That error is one of the demos.
    Proposed
    Fix the words that need attention by choosing an alternative or repeating just that phrase. Select any phrase yourself, or tap one Flow marked “Check this phrase.” Marks are optional and dismissible; only the selected span changes. iPhone and Android previews.
    Next step
    Time it against today's mobile keyboards. That comparison waits until I've observed them on a phone.
    Open Phrase RepairTry the “fix trip” demo

    Journal Mode

    Capture with minimal cleanup, polish afterward

    Idea
    Capture your thoughts in your own words. Clean them up when you're ready.
    My test
    My journal comparison largely kept my tone and story details. So this is about choice, not a fix for lost personality.
    Proposed
    Turn it on for one recording (triple-press fn, or a button), then keep the capture or polish a separate copy. Both versions stay.
    Open question
    Flow already uses a third quick tap to cancel hands-free dictation, so the shortcut needs Wispr's input.
    Open Journal Mode

    Review Revisions

    Inspect final choices, revisit earlier alternatives

    Idea
    Check the choices you changed while speaking.
    My test
    Most corrections landed. One kept Sam on a guest list I'd meant to leave Sam off; one wrote “the smaller group” without naming it.
    Proposed
    An optional control after dictations with revisions: one card per decision, change any one, preview, apply, undo.
    Replaces
    Thought Check. My scripted passages mostly kept their meaning, so a broad omission check had little to catch.
    Open Review Revisions
    4 · A larger proposalHypothesis: no direct customer evidence yet

    Voice Skills

    Repeatable professional workflows, run by voice

    Idea
    Turn a workflow you repeat into a named skill. Invoke it by voice, supply anything that changes, and let Flow follow your saved process across connected tools.
    Builds on
    Flow's Snippets, which already save repeated text behind a spoken trigger. A few reviews ask for Snippets you can type and browse, and for a list of commands. None asks for cross-tool workflows.
    Proposed
    After three accepted runs of the same process, Flow offers to save it. You review the draft, then say “slash morning, focus on onboarding.” Connections and sample data are simulated.

    Where the evidence comes from

    The Wispr Flow app reviews dashboard: 7,007 public Google Play and App Store reviews of the mobile apps, May 2025 to Oct 2026, each labeled by an LLM and checked against 120 hand-labeled reviews. Plus my own tests in the real Flow app.

    Review quotes are verbatim and identified by review ID. Store reviews are self-selected, so they show that a problem exists for someone, not how common it is. My tests are one speaker and aren't yet checked against their recordings.

    What's simulated

    • No prototype records or recognizes speech. Dictations, “Say it again” replacements and the journal entry are prepared text.
    • Phrase Repair's “Check this phrase” marks and their alternatives are demo data, not a confidence score.
    • Voice Skills' connections, task history, discovery and every email, message, number and pull request are fictional sample data.
    • Journal Mode's Polished version is written by hand, not produced by Flow or any model.
    • Review Revisions' cards and earlier choices are prepared. Scenario A is rebuilt from what I reported; B and C use scripted input.
    • The research workspace simulates nothing: every output in it is pasted from the real Flow app.
