I dictate with Flow every day. Here are four moments where my voice still stops short, and what I'd build so it doesn't.

Four moments, one idea

Your voice does the work. You stay in control. One “Edit a phrase” panel runs through all four.

  1. 01 When the work is a routine Voice Skills Say “slash morning.” Flow runs your saved routine across Gmail, Slack and Excel. Hypothesis to test →
  2. 02 When one phrase comes out wrong Phrase Repair Tap the words, pick an alternative or say just them again. Nothing else changes. Strongest evidence →
  3. 03 When you change your mind mid-sentence Review Revisions Check the choices you changed while speaking, and fix one without touching the rest. Seen in my tests →
  4. 04 When the words are the point Journal Mode Capture a thought in your own words. Polish it later, if you want. Preference to test →

How we'd know it worked

Test each with users first. If it ships, one number decides it, and every number rolls up to one Flow-level goal.

No Wispr data was available, so there are no baselines; thresholds on each page are proposed. The Flow-level goal is my assumption.

What's real and what's simulated
  • Real: review quotes (verbatim, by review ID), Wispr docs quotes (re-read Oct 16, 2026), and my own test outputs in the Research page.
  • Simulated: no prototype records or recognizes speech. Dictations, replacements, marks, the journal entry and its Polish are prepared. Voice Skills' connections, history and every email, number and pull request are fictional.
  • Not yet done: testing with users, and comparing with Flow's current mobile keyboards on a phone.
