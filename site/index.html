I dictate with Flow every day. Four moments still send me back to the keyboard. Here's what I'd build.

Real customer data, analyzed7,007 public app reviews, labeled and charted · open the dashboard ↗
  • Who it's for

    People who write by voice all day: texting on a phone, drafting email, journaling.

  • The problem

    Flow gets most words right. Fixing the rest, or keeping your own words, still takes the keyboard.

  • Why it matters

    872 reviews say saving time is why they use Flow. Every typed fix gives some of that time back.

The work behind it

Customer data first, then tests in the real app, then the prototypes.

  • 7,007app reviews parsed and labeled
  • 22categories, with 2,079 complaints sorted into them
  • 10passages dictated and checked in the real app
  • 26day streak on Flow, ~50k words (self-reported)
  • 4working prototypes, one shared repair panel

Four people, four prototypes

Your voice does the work. You stay in control.

  1. The routine runner A PM or ops lead who rebuilds the same morning brief from Gmail, Slack and Excel. 0reviews ask for this yet. My biggest bet, to test What I'd buildVoice SkillsSay “slash morning.” Flow runs the saved routine. Hypothesis to test →
  2. The phone-first texter Dictates messages all day. One wrong name sends them back to the keyboard. 247reviews on keyboard and app switching, 93 at 1 or 2 stars What I'd buildPhrase RepairTap the words, pick a fix or say them again. Strongest evidence →
  3. The out-loud thinker Drafts long emails by talking, and changes their mind mid-sentence. Leo + SamI said keep Leo, drop Sam. Flow kept both What I'd buildReview RevisionsCheck the choices you changed, fix one alone. Seen in my tests →
  4. The voice journaler Speaks to think. Wants their own words kept, not polished. 101complaints about AI editing; some say cleanup changed their meaning What I'd buildJournal ModeCapture it raw. Polish later, if you want. Preference to test →

Personas are archetypes drawn from public reviews and my own use, not from interviews.

How we'd know it worked

Test each with users first. If it ships, one number decides it, and every number rolls up to one Flow-level goal.

No Wispr data was available, so there are no baselines; thresholds on each page are proposed. The Flow-level goal is my assumption.

What's real and what's simulated
  • Real: review quotes (verbatim, by review ID), Wispr docs quotes (re-read Oct 16, 2026), and my own test outputs in the Research page.
  • Simulated: no prototype records or recognizes speech. Dictations, replacements, marks, the journal entry and its Polish are prepared. Voice Skills' connections, history and every email, number and pull request are fictional.
  • Not yet done: testing with users, and comparing with Flow's current mobile keyboards on a phone.
