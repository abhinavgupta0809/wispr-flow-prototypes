Journal Mode

Capture with minimal cleanup, polish afterward if you want

Capture your thoughts in your own words. Clean them up when you're ready.

1 · Why

A preference for control, not a fix for lost personality

Preference hypothesis

When people journal or think aloud, some would rather keep the way they said it: the hedges, the restarts, the word they'd never write. Then decide later whether to tidy it. If that's true, they need a way to say “keep it raw this time” before they speak, and a polish step they choose afterwards.

Untested. The test below measures which version people keep and whether they report edits they didn't want. It doesn't claim better transcription.

My testNot yet verified

Flow mostly kept my voice

In my journal comparison, the output largely kept my tone and the story's details. So this isn't a claim that Flow strips personality. It's a proposal to let people choose how much cleanup a recording gets. Evidence record R2

2 · What Flow ships today

Flow already has cleanup controls and Transforms on desktop

Journal Mode builds on these. What's new is a switch for one recording, and keeping the captured and polished versions side by side.

3 · The proposed behavior

One recording, minimal cleanup, both versions kept

Proposed
  • Turn it on before you speak. Triple-press fn (proposed shortcut), or click Journal Mode.
  • Always visible. A “Journal Mode · Minimal cleanup” indicator, shown apart from the microphone status, so “which mode” and “is it listening” never blur.
  • This recording only. When it ends, Flow goes back to normal capture and says so.
  • After recording: Keep this version, Polish, or Edit a phrase.
  • Polish makes a second version. Labeled Captured and Polished. Compare them, keep one, switch back. The capture is never silently replaced.
Proposed capture rules

Keep what makes it yours. Add only punctuation and paragraph breaks.

    This is the intended behavior, not a promise of word-for-word recognition. A word that couldn't be made out stays marked instead of guessed.

        5 · Open question

        Triple-press fn already means something in Flow

        Wispr docsConflicts with the proposed shortcut

        Flow's hands-free docs say a quick double-tap of the push-to-talk key starts hands-free dictation:

        And a third quick tap has its own meaning:

        So a triple-press of fn would start hands-free and then cancel it, unless Flow changes that gesture. I've kept triple-press in the prototype because it's the shortcut being proposed. It's labeled proposed, and the visible Journal Mode button is the dependable path. A dedicated binding (fn plus another key) or a setting may fit better. That needs Wispr's input.         6 · How we'd know it worked

        Measure preference and unwanted edits, not transcription

        Journal Mode is a preference hypothesis, so the test asks which version people keep and why. It makes no claim that recognition gets better.

        7 · Scope boundary

        What this prototype does not show

        • It doesn't record or recognize speech. The entry is prepared text, two lines of it quoted from my own journal comparison.
        • It doesn't show how Flow's cleanup would treat this entry with Journal Mode off.
        • The Polished version is written by hand. A real Polish would come from Flow's Transforms.
        • It holds one entry, on desktop. Mobile activation isn't designed here.
        • It has not been tested with users.
