1 · The problem

Reviewers praise the dictation, then describe the edit

Mobile users can enjoy Flow's dictation quality but lose the time-saving benefit when correcting a phrase requires awkward selection, typing, or switching keyboards. Three recent iOS reviews from the reviews dashboard export show the problem exists for some people. They don't say how many. Of the proposals here, this one has the most customer evidence behind it.

My testNot yet verified

It happened to me too. I said “Mobile Fix Strip” and Flow wrote “mobile fix trip.” Fixing those words on a phone shouldn't mean re-dictating the sentence. That error is the product-term demo below. Evidence record R7

2 · What Flow ships today

Flow already edits by voice on desktop. On iPhone, in-place editing isn't currently working

This proposal is about repairing a phrase on mobile, not voice editing in general.

Not yet observed

The phone comparison waits for a device check

I have read Wispr's docs, not used the current mobile keyboards for this task. Nothing here compares with them until it's recorded on a phone.

  1. Dictate “Send the proposal to Risha.” into Messages with the Flow keyboard, on iPhone and on Android.
  2. Change “Risha” to “Rishav” the fastest way each app allows. Note each tap, keyboard switch and the seconds taken.
  3. Check whether either keyboard offers alternatives, a voice edit or Polish on a one-word selection. iOS Polish's documented minimum is 10 words.
  4. Record app versions, OS versions and date. Then add the comparison.

3 · The proposed behavior

Repair by hand, or check what Flow marked

Manual correction
  • Select any word or phrase in your latest dictation. It appears as tappable words right under the message, so the message stays in view.
  • One compact repair panel: suggested alternatives when there are any, Say it again, and Type replacement.
  • Repeat just that phrase. The new words are shown in place before anything changes.
  • Exact replacement. Only the selected span changes. Nothing is regenerated, and Undo puts the old text back.
Suggested review
  • A subtle underline on a phrase that may be wrong, labeled “Check this phrase.” It covers exactly the words that need attention: one word or several.
  • Tap it to open the same repair panel. Choose an alternative, repeat the phrase, or Keep as written.
  • Never a gate. You can send with a mark still showing.

Simulated In this prototype, every mark and alternative is written into the demo data. It doesn't show that Flow can measure word-level confidence. That would need its own evaluation: how often a mark is right, and how often real errors go unmarked.

    5 · Feasibility

    The same interaction on both platforms; other apps are a separate question

    Needs a native check

    The prototype edits text inside its own composer. In a real keyboard on iOS or Android, the repair panel would have to find the latest dictated span in another app's text field and replace exactly that range, even after the user or autocorrect has moved the cursor. Whether each platform's keyboard APIs allow that reliably is untested here. It's a feasibility requirement to settle before building, not something this demo shows.

    6 · How we'd know it worked

    Test it against today's keyboards first

    Nothing here has been timed with users, so nothing claims the repair is faster. First a usability test, then, if it ships, the north star: time to a correct, send-ready message.

    7 · Scope boundary

    What this prototype does not show

    • It does not work in other apps (see Feasibility).
    • It does not recognize speech or detect uncertain words. Marks, alternatives and repeats are prepared.
    • It handles one repair at a time, with one level of undo.
    • It has not been tested with users.
