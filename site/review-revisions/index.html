Steps

    Simulated

    Each scenario's decisions are written by hand, and the message is built from them. The excerpts are the scripted input, labeled as demo data. A real version would take them from the transcript or the audio Flow already keeps under the user's settings, and could only offer choices that are actually in it.

    No confidence scores. If the source doesn't settle a choice, the card says Unresolved and you decide.

    My test one speaker, my own Flow app Reconstruction rebuilt from what I reported, not from a checked recording Proposed my idea, not a Flow feature Scripted prepared input and cards, no recognition

    Why

    Most spoken corrections land. The ones that don't are hard to spot

    My ten scripted passages mostly kept their meaning, so a general “did anything get dropped?” check had little to catch. What did go wrong was narrower: choices I changed mid-sentence.

    My testNot yet verified
    • Several spoken corrections resolved correctly.
    • Guest list, second try: I meant to keep Leo and drop Sam. The output kept both. (In the first try, adding Sam back was on purpose.)
    • A complex scheduling dictation kept the final date and time, but wrote “the smaller group” without saying who was in it.

    One speaker, my own runs, not yet checked against the recordings. Evidence records R3 to R6

    Proposed
    • Optional, never a gate. Normal dictation goes straight in. A small Review revisions control appears only when the dictation contained revisions.
    • One card per decision: guests, date, time, conditions. Each shows what the message says now.
    • Three kinds of choice: clearly replaced, still unresolved, and a condition that still applies.
    • Change one decision at a time. Restoring an earlier day doesn't bring back the earlier time.
    • Preview, then apply. Cancel, undo and the original output stay available.

    How we'd know it worked

    Compare it with simply dictating another correction

    The obvious alternative is saying “Actually, drop Sam.” Review Revisions earns its place only if its controls make the final message more correct, with less effort.

    Scope boundary

    What this prototype does not show
    • It doesn't detect revisions. Whether a model can reliably find the decisions in a dictation, and their earlier values, is the main open question.
    • Scenario A is rebuilt from what I reported. Its recording hasn't been checked, so it shows no spoken excerpt and no earlier names.
    • Scenarios B and C use scripted input from the brief, not recordings.
    • It has not been tested with users.
