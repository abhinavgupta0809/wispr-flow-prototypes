Thought Check

When spoken thoughts become a cleaned-up draft, users may struggle to notice whether an important detail, condition, or instruction disappeared or changed.

1 · The hypothesis

Test first: does the draft keep what was said?

Some reviewers say cleanup changes or drops what they said. That shows the worry exists. It doesn't show how often it happens, or whether people miss it. So the experiment comes before any claim about the feature's value.

3 · The experiment

Ten scripted passages, read into the real Flow app

Method

  1. Set the conditions below once. Keep device, microphone, app version and cleanup settings the same for all ten runs.
  2. Read each passage aloud into the Flow app, exactly as written. Record the audio separately (voice memo or screen recording).
  3. Paste Flow's output into Actual Flow output without editing it, then lock it.
  4. If Flow shows a pre-cleanup transcript, paste it too. If not, leave it empty: discrepancies are then end-to-end.
  5. Mark each meaning unit. Rewording, removed fillers and legitimate self-corrections are not errors.
  6. Misread the script? Mark the run invalid and add a redo. The invalid run stays here.
  7. After all ten, repeat each passage that needed substantive correction, to see if it recurs.
  8. Then record the two unscripted brain dumps and assess them against their recordings.

This workspace never transcribes anything. Every output here is pasted from the real app.

Boundaries the interaction will keep

  • The scripted passages grade the experiment. They never feed the interaction.
  • Suggestions may only come from evidence a product could hold: captured audio or an available transcript of it.
  • It can't recover words the microphone never captured.
  • Suggestions in the demo are prepared by hand and labeled simulated. No confidence scores, no claim of automatic detection.
  • The pasted Flow output stays untouched; the demo edits a separate copy.
